Learning science

Explore the research-informed study techniques and limits of Uni Lecture Tutor.

The tutor draws on learning research to structure practice, feedback and revision. It has not been independently evaluated for learning outcomes; the research below supports teaching techniques, not claims about this product.

01

Retrieval practice

Recall studied material before looking at the answer.

02

Active recall

Explain a concept or solve a problem from memory.

03

Spaced repetition

Return to important ideas across separate sessions.

04

Successive relearning

Practise until successful, then retrieve again in later sessions.

05

Worked examples

Study a complete solution before attempting a similar problem.

06

Scaffolding and fading

Start with guidance and reduce it as you become independent.

07

Self-explanation

Explain why each step works in your own words.

08

Interleaving

Mix appropriate problem types to practise selecting the right approach.

09

Cognitive load management

Break complex material into meaningful steps.

10

Knowledge transfer

Apply knowledge to unfamiliar examples and situations.

11

Discipline-specific pedagogy

Use the reasoning, representations and checks appropriate to the subject.

Why past-paper practice can support learning

Retrieval before reveal, actionable feedback, worked examples with fading, distributed practice and transfer tasks inform the design. Descriptive historical recurrence is not exam prediction. Assisted practice is not evidence of independent mastery. No product efficacy claim is made.
